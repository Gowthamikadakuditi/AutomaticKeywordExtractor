1. Project Abstract & Objectives

With the exponential growth of unstructured digital documents across research repositories, technical documentation, and news media, automatic keyword and keyphrase extraction serves as a fundamental natural language processing (NLP) task. This project provides a production-grade, student-friendly platform capable of analyzing academic research papers, news reports, and technical manuals to extract salient topical terms without requiring manual human curation.

Primary Objectives
  • Automate identification of salient single-word keywords and multi-word keyphrases.
  • Provide multi-format document ingestion for PDF, DOCX, and TXT files.
  • Deliver statistical (YAKE and RAKE) and vector-space (TF-IDF) keyword extraction algorithms.
  • Present real-time visual analytics via Chart.js and multi-format exports.
Existing vs Proposed

Existing: Manual tagging is slow, biased, and does not scale. Simple word clouds show frequent words rather than informative key terms.
Proposed: Unsupervised statistical & linguistic pipelines that assess casing, position, co-occurrence, and sentence-level distinctiveness.

2. Problem Statement

Given an unstructured text document \( D \), develop an NLP system that extracts a ranked subset of \( K \) terms \( \{k_1, k_2, \dots, k_K\} \) that most accurately represent the topical essence, technical contributions, and salient concepts of \( D \), while filtering out non-topical function words, boilerplate vocabulary, and noise.

3. Keywords vs. Keyphrases

Dimension Keyword (Unigram) Keyphrase (Multi-word N-gram)
Definition A single lexical word expressing a central topic. A contiguous sequence of 2 or more words acting as a cohesive noun phrase.
Example transformers, attention, extraction natural language processing, self-attention mechanism
Semantic Clarity Can be ambiguous out of context (e.g. "learning"). Highly specific and contextually unambiguous (e.g. "deep reinforcement learning").
Linguistic Form Single Noun or Verb. Noun phrase chunks: \(\text{Adjective}^* \text{Noun}^+\).

5. Deep-Dive into Extraction Algorithms

Method 1: YAKE (Yet Another Keyword Extractor)
Statistical • Unsupervised

YAKE is a lightweight, unsupervised statistical keyword extractor that operates purely on single documents without requiring a pre-trained background corpus or deep neural network. It calculates five text features for candidate terms:

  1. Casing: Evaluates capitalized terms and acronyms vs lowercase words.
  2. Word Positional Bias: Prioritizes words appearing early in the document or introductory sentences.
  3. Word Frequency & Relatedness: Balances term frequency against context richness.
  4. Dispersion across Sentences: Evaluates how uniformly or selectively a word appears across sentences.
Score Semantics: In YAKE, a lower raw score signifies a higher importance (0 is an ideal score). For intuitive visualization, our platform maps the raw score \( S_{raw} \) to a normalized relevance score:
\( \text{Relevance} = \frac{1}{1 + S_{raw}} \in (0, 1] \)
Method 2: TF-IDF (Sentence-Level Pseudo-Corpus)
Vector Space • Scikit-Learn

Standard TF-IDF computes: \( \text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \log\left(\frac{1 + |D|}{1 + \text{DF}(t, D)}\right) + 1 \) Academic Honesty Note on Single-Document TF-IDF: In a single document, treating the whole document as a solitary corpus yields a flat, constant IDF value across all observed terms. To overcome this limitation sensibly, our engine treats the document's constituent sentences as a pseudo-corpus:

  • Inverse Sentence Frequency (ISF): Terms that occur in every sentence are penalized as generic structural filler.
  • Topical Concentrates: Words that concentrate heavily in topical paragraphs or core sentences receive elevated TF-IDF weights.
  • Scores are aggregated via maximum sentence activation combined with mean sentence activation, normalized to [0, 1].

6. Evaluation Methodology (Viva Q&A)

In an academic evaluation, automatic keyword extractors are evaluated against gold-standard human-annotated keyword sets (e.g. Inspec, SemEval-2010, Krapivin datasets). The standard evaluation metrics are calculated at rank cutoff \( K \):

Precision@K

Proportion of extracted top-K items that are true keywords.

\( P@K = \frac{|E_K \cap G|}{K} \)
Recall@K

Proportion of all true gold-standard keywords discovered in top-K.

\( R@K = \frac{|E_K \cap G|}{|G|} \)
F1-Score@K

Harmonic mean of Precision@K and Recall@K.

\( F_1 = \frac{2 \times P@K \times R@K}{P@K + R@K} \)
Examiner Viva Tip: Do not claim an arbitrary "95% accuracy" for unsupervised keyword extraction without an annotated benchmark dataset. State that unsupervised extraction is evaluated on public corpora (like SemEval-2010) where state-of-the-art unsupervised methods typically achieve F1-scores between 0.35 and 0.48 due to linguistic variability in human annotator choices.
